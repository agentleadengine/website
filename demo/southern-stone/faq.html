Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about stone and tile restoration. Don't see yours? Call or send a message. Advice is free.

Is restoration really cheaper than replacing my countertops?

Almost always. New marble and granite run roughly $75 to $200 or more per square foot installed. Restoration works with the stone you already own, so you get the like-new result without the demolition, fabrication, and installation costs.

My marble has dull spots and rings. Is it ruined?

Those are usually etches: marks where something acidic (wine, citrus, cleaners) ate into the polish. Etching looks alarming but it lives in the surface, not the stone. Honing and repolishing removes it in most cases.

What surfaces do you work on?

Marble, granite, travertine, slate, limestone, terrazzo, and ceramic or porcelain tile with grout: countertops, floors, showers, foyers, and outdoor stone like patios and pool decks.

How long does a typical job take?

It depends on the surface and its condition, which is why we start with an assessment. Most residential jobs are measured in hours or days, not weeks, and you'll know the scope and the price before we start.

Do I need to seal my stone? How often?

Most natural stone is porous and benefits from a penetrating sealer. How often depends on the stone and how the surface is used. The quick check: if water stops beading and starts darkening the stone, it's due.

Should I choose marble or granite?

Both are beautiful, durable natural stones. The difference is character and temperament. Marble carries veining and reads softer, but it's more sensitive to acids and etching. Granite is more granular, harder-wearing, and more forgiving in a busy kitchen. Either way, maintenance is what makes the investment last.
