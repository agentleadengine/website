How to clean and maintain natural stone

Stone is durable, but it isn't invincible. The right habits, on the right schedule, keep marble and granite looking the way they did the day they were installed.

The habits that protect your stone

  • Wipe spills right away. Wine, coffee, citrus, and tomato etch marble in minutes.
  • Use a pH-neutral cleaner. Vinegar, bleach, and bathroom sprays dull polished stone.
  • Use cutting boards and trivets. Knives scratch, and hot pans shock the sealer.
  • Dust-mop floors before wet cleaning. Grit is what actually scratches stone floors.
  • Mind the entryways. Walk-off mats catch the sand that grinds down the finish.

Why sealing matters

Most natural stone is porous. A penetrating sealer doesn't change the look of the stone. It buys you time when something spills. Sealers wear off with use, which is why counters and showers need resealing on a schedule, not just once at installation.

The simple test: drip a little water on the counter. If it darkens the stone within a few minutes instead of beading up, the sealer is due.

A maintenance plan, not a rescue mission

To extend the lifespan of your marble or granite as long as possible, the smart move is a care schedule: periodic deep cleaning, resealing, and small repairs before they grow. We set these up for our clients so a small habit never becomes a replacement project.
