Stone care notes

Short, practical notes from the trade: what actually keeps stone and tile looking new between visits.

Marble · Care basics

Why your marble went dull (and why it isn't ruined)

Nine times out of ten, dull marble isn't worn out. It's etched. Marble is calcium carbonate, and anything acidic dissolves a microscopic layer of the polish on contact. Lemon juice, wine, tomato sauce, vinegar-based cleaners: each leaves a flat, cloudy mark where the shine used to be.

The good news is that etching lives in the finish, not the stone. Honing takes the surface down past the damage and repolishing brings the gloss back. The habit that prevents it: wipe acids fast, and clean with a pH-neutral stone soap instead of whatever is under the sink.

Grout · Cleaning

The reason your grout never looks clean

Grout is cement: porous, rough, and happy to hold onto every mopping's dirty water. Regular mopping actually darkens grout over time: the mop lifts dirt from the tile and deposits it in the lines. That's why floors look better everywhere except the grid.

Real grout cleaning works the opposite way: the line gets agitated, then the dirty water is extracted out of the pores instead of pushed back in. Once it's clean, a grout sealer keeps the next year of mop water from soaking in.

Sealing · Maintenance

The thirty-second sealer test

Stone sealers wear off quietly. There's no line on the calendar, and the stone looks the same right up until something stains. The test costs nothing: drip a spoonful of water on the counter and wait a few minutes. Beads on top? Sealed. Darkening ring spreading into the stone? The sealer is done, and the next glass of red wine is a permanent souvenir.

Run the test a couple of times a year on kitchen counters and shower walls, the two places sealers die fastest.
