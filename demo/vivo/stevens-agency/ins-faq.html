Auto Insurance

State law sets the minimum liability limits, but the legal minimum is rarely enough. If you cause a serious accident, minimum coverage can leave you personally responsible for costs that exceed your policy limits. We recommend at least 100/300/100 liability coverage, meaning $100,000 per person, $300,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $100,000 for property damage. If you have a newer or financed vehicle, adding comprehensive and collision is also essential. One of our agents will walk you through the right coverage for your specific situation, free of charge.
Liability-only coverage pays for damage and injuries you cause to other people in an at-fault accident. It does not cover damage to your own vehicle. Full coverage adds two protections: comprehensive (theft, vandalism, weather damage, hitting an animal) and collision (damage to your car in a crash, regardless of fault). If your vehicle is financed or leased, your lender will almost certainly require full coverage. If you own your car outright, the decision comes down to your vehicle's value and your ability to absorb the cost of a loss.

For at-fault accidents, most carriers apply a surcharge for 3 to 5 years. Before filing a claim for a minor incident, especially one close to your deductible amount, call us first. We'll help you calculate whether filing makes financial sense or whether paying out of pocket protects your record better long-term.

Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage protects you when a driver who hits you either has no insurance at all, or doesn't have enough coverage to pay for your injuries and vehicle damage. Roughly 1 in 7 drivers on U.S. roads is uninsured, and even insured drivers may carry the state minimum, which often isn't enough. UM/UIM is one of the most important and most underestimated coverages on any auto policy. We always recommend including it.
There are several effective strategies: bundle your auto and home policies with the same carrier, maintain a clean driving record, complete a state-approved defensive driving course, add multiple vehicles to one policy, raise your deductible if you can afford to cover it out of pocket, and ask about good student or safe driver app discounts. As an independent agency, we compare 15+ carriers every time we quote you, so you're always getting the most competitive rate for your specific profile, not just the first option.

Home Insurance

No, and this surprises many homeowners. Standard homeowner's insurance covers sudden, accidental water damage from inside your home (like a burst pipe or overflowing appliance), but it does not cover flooding from outside. Heavy rain, storm surge, overflowing rivers, and groundwater seepage are all excluded. Separate flood insurance is available through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) and some private carriers. Even if you're not in a designated flood zone, flooding can and does happen. Ask us about your risk level and flood coverage options.
Replacement cost coverage pays to rebuild your home or replace belongings at today's prices, with no deduction for depreciation. Actual cash value (ACV) pays the current depreciated value, meaning a 10-year-old roof gets paid out at a fraction of its replacement cost. The difference at claim time can easily be tens of thousands of dollars. Replacement cost coverage carries a slightly higher premium, but we almost always recommend it. When disaster strikes, you want to be made whole, not partially reimbursed.
In most cases, no. Standard homeowner's policies severely limit coverage for business equipment (often $2,500 or less) and provide zero liability coverage for business activities. If a client or delivery person is injured at your home during business hours, or if a client sues you over a professional error, your homeowner's policy won't respond. If you run any business from home, even part-time or as a side income, you need at minimum a home-based business endorsement, and possibly a full business owner's policy. Let us review your situation.
Your dwelling coverage should reflect what it would cost to completely rebuild your home from the ground up at today's construction prices, not its market value or what you paid for it. These numbers can be very different. We use replacement cost estimators to calculate the right amount based on your home's size, construction type, and local labor costs. We also recommend reviewing your policy every year, since material and labor costs have risen significantly in recent years. An under-insured home is a major financial risk at claim time.

Business Insurance

Yes. Your homeowner's policy almost certainly excludes business activities, limits business equipment coverage to a very low amount, and provides no liability protection for business-related incidents. If a client visits your home and is injured, or if you're sued over a product or professional service you provided, you'll be exposed with no coverage. Whether you're a full-time freelancer, a part-time consultant, or running an online store, you need proper business coverage. A home-based business endorsement or business owner's policy is often more affordable than people expect.
General liability (GL) protects your business from claims of bodily injury (a customer slips at your location), property damage (you accidentally damage a client's property while working), and personal and advertising injury (claims of defamation or copyright infringement in your marketing). It covers legal defense costs, settlements, and judgments. Most commercial landlords require it before you sign a lease, and many larger clients require a certificate of insurance before hiring you. It's the cornerstone of any business insurance program.
Requirements vary by state, but most states require workers' compensation coverage once you have one or more employees. It covers medical expenses, rehabilitation costs, and a portion of lost wages when an employee is injured on the job, and it protects your business from lawsuits related to those injuries. Even in states where it's technically optional for very small employers, operating without it is a serious financial gamble. We'll verify your state's exact requirements and find you the right coverage. Many of our clients are surprised by how affordable workers' comp can be.
A Business Owner's Policy (BOP) bundles general liability coverage and commercial property coverage into a single, cost-effective policy. It's designed for small to mid-sized businesses and typically costs significantly less than purchasing each coverage separately. Most BOPs also include business interruption coverage, which pays your lost income if a covered event forces you to temporarily close. If your business has a physical location, business equipment or inventory, and any customer-facing operations, a BOP is usually the smartest and most economical starting point. We'll let you know if your business qualifies.

Most carriers provide a grace period of 10 to 30 days depending on your state and policy type. During that grace period, your coverage remains active. After the grace period, your policy can be cancelled for non-payment, and a lapse in coverage can raise your future rates significantly. If you're having difficulty making a payment, call us before the due date. We can often help you explore options like payment deferrals or carrier assistance programs before cancellation occurs. Catching a problem early always costs less than fixing it after the fact.
