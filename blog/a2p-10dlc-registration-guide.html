A2P 10DLC Registration: A Practical Guide for Insurance Agencies

If your agency texts clients or leads from a regular local number through any kind of software, US carriers require A2P 10DLC registration. Skip it and your texts get blocked, not just filtered. Here is the whole process in plain English: what to submit, what it costs in 2026, how long it really takes, and the mistakes that get insurance agencies rejected.

THE BASICS

What A2P 10DLC Actually Is

A2P stands for application to person. 10DLC stands for 10 digit long code, a normal local phone number. Put together, A2P 10DLC is the carrier framework for business texting sent through software from local numbers. Any text that leaves a CRM, an agency management system, a rater follow up tool, or a texting platform counts as A2P, even if a human typed it. The carriers built a central database called The Campaign Registry, or TCR, so they know who is sending what. You register once through your messaging provider, and AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon all read from the same record.

Registered traffic gets delivered

Registered senders get higher throughput and far less spam filtering. Carriers know who you are and what you send, so your renewal reminders actually arrive.

Unregistered traffic gets blocked

Major US carriers now block unregistered A2P traffic on local numbers outright. This stopped being a warning-and-fees situation a while ago. No registration, no delivery.

Two layers, both required

You register a brand, which is your agency, and then at least one campaign, which is a messaging use case. We break down the difference in our brand vs campaign guide.

STEP 1

Brand Registration: Proving Who You Are

The brand is your legal business entity. Your messaging provider submits it to TCR, and TCR checks it against government records. This step is mostly automated, so it usually clears in minutes to a couple of days. Gather these four things before you start and it goes smoothly.

EIN and exact legal name

The legal name must match what the IRS has on file for your EIN, character for character. Copy it from your EIN letter, not from memory or your storefront sign.

Business address and website

A real address and a working website on your own domain. A parked domain or a Facebook page in place of a site will cause problems later at campaign review.

Vertical and entity type

Insurance is its own vertical category. Pick it. Also select the correct entity type, such as LLC or corporation, matching your state registration.

Contact details

A business email on your domain and a phone number someone actually answers. Free email addresses lower your trust score with vetting providers.

STEP 2

Campaign Registration: Explaining What You Send

The campaign describes a specific use case, like customer care or marketing, and this is where the manual review happens. A human at a vetting provider reads your submission and can reject it. Your provider will ask for a use case description, sample messages, and a description of how people opt in. Treat each field as evidence. The reviewer wants to see that a real business collects real consent and sends what it says it sends. Your sample messages should look like your actual texts, include your agency name, and include opt out wording. Your opt in description should say exactly where consent is collected, usually a quote form on your website, and that page needs the consent language live before you submit. We are not lawyers, so have your attorney sign off on your final consent wording.

TIMELINE

How Long It Really Takes

Plan for about two weeks end to end and be pleasantly surprised if it is faster. The single biggest factor is whether your first submission is clean, because every rejection restarts the clock.

Brand: minutes to 2 days

Mostly automated. Delays here almost always trace back to an EIN and legal name mismatch or a typo in the address.

Campaign: 1 to 5 business days

Clean submissions often clear carrier review in 24 to 72 hours. Vetting is manual, so 3 to 5 business days is still normal even when nothing is wrong.

Rejections: weeks

A rejected campaign has to be corrected and resubmitted to the back of the queue. Two or three rejection cycles can stretch the whole process past a month.

REJECTIONS

Why Insurance Campaigns Get Rejected

Insurance sits close to lead generation in the eyes of reviewers, so agency submissions get read carefully. These are the rejection reasons we see most.

No consent language on your forms

Reviewers visit your website and look for TCPA consent wording next to your quote form. If it is missing, you get rejected. Fix the form first, then submit.

Sample messages too generic

"Hi, this is your agent with an update" tells the reviewer nothing. Write samples that match your use case: renewal reminders, quote follow ups, claim check ins, with your agency name in them.

Missing STOP and HELP wording

Opt out support has to show up in your opt in language and in at least one sample message. Reply STOP to opt out, reply HELP for help.

Lead generation framing

Language about buying leads, contacting prospects on behalf of partners, or third party consent raises flags fast. Describe consent your agency collects directly.

Public URL shorteners

Links like bit.ly in sample messages are associated with spam and get campaigns declined. Use your own domain in every link.

Website problems

A site that is down, under construction, or missing a privacy policy fails review. The site needs to clearly belong to the brand you registered.

WHILE YOU WAIT

What to Do While Your Campaign Is in Review

Do not text from the unregistered number while you wait. It will not deliver reliably, and it can hurt the number's reputation before you even start. Use the review window to get the rest of your follow up in order. Put the consent language on every lead form, not just the one you referenced in the submission. Clean your contact list and separate people who gave written consent from people who did not, because only the first group should get marketing texts. Work your leads by phone and email in the meantime, since neither channel needs 10DLC. And draft your first templates now, with your agency name and opt out wording built in, so you can start the day you are approved.
